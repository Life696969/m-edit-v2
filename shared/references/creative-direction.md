# Creative-direction system

Understand the video before decorating it. The edit should make the message clearer, more credible, better paced, or more emotionally precise.

## Establish the brief

Identify:

- target viewer and likely viewing context
- platform, aspect ratio, duration constraints, and delivery requirements
- purpose and desired viewer response
- opening question, hook, image, or action
- promise, argument, story, demonstration, or payoff
- proof and credibility moments
- ending, conclusion, or call to action
- sections likely to lose attention
- source limitations: framing, audio, lighting, continuity, screen legibility, and missing assets

When key information is missing, infer only what the footage and request support. Mark assumptions in the guide.

## Classify every clip

Useful roles include opening, setup, explanation, demonstration, comparison, list, example, proof, emotional turn, reaction, transition, conclusion, and call to action. A clip can have more than one role, but the plan should name its primary job.

## Visuals by function

Choose visual treatment based on what the line needs:

- comprehension: diagrams, labels, step cards, screen crops
- proof: source screenshots, before/after, demonstrated result
- comparison: side-by-side, table, aligned cards
- pacing: crop change, scene transition, full-screen visual, intentional pause
- emotion: framing, whitespace, quieter motion, sound restraint
- continuity: match cuts, motion direction, color and spacing consistency

Possible formats include clean talking head, crop change, split screen, screen recording, diagram, comparison card, checklist, timeline, highlighted quote, product image, generated graphic, or brief supporting b-roll.

Do not force every technique into one video. Purposeful variation inside one visual system is stronger than constant novelty.

## Pacing

Remove accidental dead air only when meaning and natural delivery remain intact. Let intentional pauses breathe. Place visual resets at topic shifts, new evidence, changes in energy, or moments where the viewer must reorient. Do not add resets on an arbitrary timer when the content does not support them.

m-edit's **camera pulse** (`camera-pulse.md`) is not a visual reset. It is a felt-not-seen camera rhythm, governed by `edit_rules.camera_pulse_seconds`, and cards and cutaways still follow the content.

## Coherence

Define typography, caption treatment, spacing, motion language, framing, asset treatment, audio approach, and transition behavior once. Reuse components and tokens, then vary composition by clip role.

## Restraint test

For every motion, graphic, sound, crop, or asset ask:

1. What viewer problem does this solve?
2. Does it compete with speech or action?
3. Is the same point already clear without it?
4. Does it fit the rest of the system?

Remove effects with no defensible answer.
